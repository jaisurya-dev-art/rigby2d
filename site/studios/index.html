What changes for your studio

No seats to count

No per-user licenses for the editor or the runtimes, no revenue tiers, no audits. Add a contractor for a month, onboard a whole team, ship ten games: the cost stays zero.

No version lock

One runtime reads skeleton JSON from 3.8 to 4.3 and binary .skel 4.3. Upgrading the editor never means re-exporting the back catalogue or swapping runtimes mid-production.

Your characters come with you

Rigby2D opens existing skeleton exports and DragonBones projects as they are: bones, meshes, weights, constraints, skins and animations. DragonBones import is checked against its own runtime.

No vendor risk

The editor and every runtime are public source. Read it, patch it, fork it. Your pipeline never depends on one company's roadmap, pricing or survival.

The same animation everywhere

Eight runtimes match the editor frame by frame, checked in public CI on every commit, so what the animator approved is what ships, on every engine and platform.

Faster character production

AutoRig turns a layered PSD or SVG into a rigged character in one step, Python scripts automate the rest, and an MCP server lets AI assistants work on real projects. Reviews happen in the browser, no install.

How studios switch

Start with one character. Nothing about the rest of the project has to change until you decide.

  1. Pick a character from a current or past project, with its skeleton export (.json or .skel) or DragonBones project.
  2. Open it in Rigby2D. The animators keep working in a familiar bones-and-slots workflow: dopesheet, graph editor, meshes, IK, skins.
  3. Swap the runtime in one scene of your game, and compare it side by side with what you ship today.
  4. Roll out project by project, at your pace.

Questions studios ask

Is it really free for commercial games?

Yes. The editor and the runtimes are open source under MIT or Apache-2.0, with no revenue limit, no per-seat fee and no royalties. Studios of any size can use them in any number of games.

Who maintains it, and what if development stops?

Rigby2D ships often, with every release built in public CI, and it is funded by its supporters. Because everything is open source, your studio keeps the full source of the editor and runtimes whatever happens: nothing can be taken away or relicensed under you.

Will our existing characters work?

Skeleton exports in JSON 3.8 to 4.3 and binary .skel 4.3 open as they are, and DragonBones projects import with their nested armatures. The migration guides list exactly what carries over. Older files can be exported again from the tool that made them.

How fast are the runtimes?

The Rust runtime poses a 143-bone character with 21 constraints in about 22 µs; the Unity runtime animates 200 characters in 5 ms a frame on a 4-core laptop. The Runtimes page has the numbers and a live test you can run in your browser.

Does it send our work anywhere?

No. The desktop app and the browser editor work on your files locally; nothing about you or your projects is sent. The privacy statement lists every network request the app makes.

Can we get support?

Write to hello@rigby2d.com, open an issue on GitHub, or ask on Discord. Studios trying Rigby2D get direct help with their first characters.
