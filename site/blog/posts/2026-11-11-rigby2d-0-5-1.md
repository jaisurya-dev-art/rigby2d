[Download 0.5.1](https://github.com/jaisurya-dev-art/rigby2d/releases/tag/v0.5.1), let the editor

## Live in the Defold editor

A Defold developer building a Metroidvania toolkit asked us a fair question: Spine models show up
in the Defold editor, so why did a Rigby2D character only appear once the game ran?

Now it doesn't. A **Rigby2D Model** is a component you add to a game object, like a sprite or a
Spine model. The Defold editor plays it right in the scene view: pick the animation and the skin in
the Properties panel, press Space, and watch it move. The preview runs the same Rigby2D runtime the
game uses, so what you see is what ships.

- **One file, one click.** *File ▸ Export ▸ Defold model…* writes everything into your Defold
  project: the skeleton, the images with a Defold atlas, the component and a game object ready to
  drag into a collection.
- **Native in the game**, driven by `rigby.play`, `rigby.queue` and `rigby.on_event`.
- **Free to ship.** No editor license, no runtime license, for you or for anyone using your toolkit.

The [Defold guide](https://github.com/jaisurya-dev-art/rigby2d/blob/main/runtimes/defold/README.md#rigby2d-models-live-in-the-editor) has the details.

## PixiJS, natively

Rigby2D characters now play in **PixiJS 8**, on WebGL and WebGPU. A skeleton is an ordinary PixiJS
container, so filters, masks, tint and pointer events just work, and it batches with your sprites.
Meshes, IK, physics, clipping and two-color tint all play exactly as in the editor. For crowds,
an optional WebAssembly core built from our Rust runtime runs about twice as fast.

## 简体中文

The whole editor is now in Simplified Chinese: every menu, panel and dialog. Pick it in
*Preferences ▸ General ▸ Language*.

## You asked, we built

Your posts on Discord and GitHub turned into these:

- **Bone colors**, to tell limbs and sides apart at a glance.
- **Hold Shift while translating** to move along one axis.
- **Zoom the timeline** with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+wheel, and **cut, copy and paste keys** from the
  right-click menu.
- **Setup and Animate tabs**, a timeline bar with frame rate, speed and timecode, and **boomerang**
  playback.
- **Undo, redo, fit and show-bones buttons** next to the mode tabs, with shortcuts in every tooltip.
- **Slots fold in the tree**, so busy rigs stay readable.
- **A pixel grid** for pixel art, and **show or hide** bones, masks, bounding boxes and paths one by
  one.
- **Ease in by default** if you like: choose the curve new keys start with.
- **Double-click a `.rigby` file** to open it, or drop files on the window.

And fixes you reported: stretching IK now reaches its target when the chain is bent, weight
painting keeps a mesh's animation, image sequences read their names correctly, and new child bones
keep their length.

## Linux

GitHub's Linux build machines couldn't reach Ubuntu's package servers today, so the Linux AppImage
and `.deb` aren't on the release yet. They'll be added as soon as they build. Windows, macOS, the
browser editor and every runtime are ready now.
