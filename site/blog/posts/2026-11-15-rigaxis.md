This is a first for Rigby2D, and we're really proud of it: **someone outside the project built a
runtime.** No request from us and no roadmap item, just a developer who wanted Rigby2D in their engine
and made it happen.

It's **[RigAxis](https://github.com/zrurf/RigAxis)** by **zrurf**, and the engine is **Unreal Engine
5**, one of the engines people ask us about most. Rigby2D is open source so that things like this can
happen, and seeing it happen five weeks in means a lot.

## What it does

RigAxis plays Rigby2D skeleton and scene exports inside Unreal Engine 5:

- **Skeletons and atlases**: `.skel`, skeleton `.json` and `.atlas`, with editor importers and
  reimport.
- **Playback**: tracks with crossfades and queues, speed and manual time, skins and physics reset.
- **Blueprints**: animation events arrive as Blueprint delegates, and a bone follower component
  attaches actors to animated bones.
- **Rendering**: its own scene proxy with normal, additive, multiply and screen blending and two-color
  tint.
- **Scenes**: Rigby2D scene manifests with parallax stages, actors, shots and camera bones.

Under the hood it bundles Rigby2D's own **Rust runtime**, the same one our Godot addon uses, so
playback follows the official runtime rather than a port.

## Built for a real game

zrurf built RigAxis for their own game, so it covers what that game needs first. Windows is tested end
to end; build scripts for Android, macOS, iOS and Linux are there too. It's an independent project,
not made or maintained by us, and its licence and issues live on its own repository.

RigAxis will be listed as a **community runtime** in our docs with the next release. If you've built
something on Rigby2D too, a runtime, a tool or a plugin, tell us: this is exactly how Rigby2D grows.

Thank you, zrurf. 🙌
