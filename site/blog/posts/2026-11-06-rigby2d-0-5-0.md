Rigby2D 0.5: our biggest update yet. A **native Defold runtime**, **sprite sheet export** drawn on the GPU, **SVG import** from Inkscape and Affinity, **generate a mesh from an image** in one click, and the **Godot runtime, production-ready**. If you have 0.4.x, the editor offers this update by itself.

[Download 0.5.0](https://github.com/jaisurya-dev-art/rigby2d/releases/tag/v0.5.0) or [open the editor in your browser](https://rigby2d.com/editor/).

## What's new

### Defold runtime

Rigby2D now runs in Defold: a native C++ runtime, on every platform Defold ships to (Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS, HTML5), matching the editor frame by frame ([guide](https://github.com/jaisurya-dev-art/rigby2d/blob/main/runtimes/defold/README.md)).

- **Add it as a library**: in *game.project ▸ Project ▸ Dependencies*, add `https://github.com/jaisurya-dev-art/rigby2d/releases/latest/download/rigby2d-defold.zip` and choose *Project ▸ Fetch Libraries*.
- **Everything plays**: meshes and weights, IK and physics, skins, events, tracks with crossfades, and Rigby2D scenes.
- Checked in CI against the reference runtime on every push.

### Sprite sheet export

*File ▸ Export ▸ Sprite sheet…* turns any animation into frames for engines and tools that play frames instead of bones.

- **Three formats**: a packed sheet with JSON (Phaser, PixiJS, TexturePacker, Aseprite tags), a Defold atlas with its frames, or PNG frames.
- **Drawn on the GPU**: 120 large frames in about 4 seconds instead of about 30.
- **Big sheets split** across several sheets within the size you choose, with JSON that Phaser and PixiJS load as one. The dialog shows the frame size and sheet count before you export, and the largest size that fits on one sheet. Power-of-two sheet sizes are an option.
- Also from the AI assistant: the `export_sprite_sheet` MCP tool.

### SVG import

*File ▸ Import ▸ SVG…* brings in a drawing from Inkscape, Affinity or Illustrator like a PSD: every layer becomes a part, placed where it was drawn, ready for AutoRig. Inkscape sublayers are parts of their own, hidden layers come in hidden, and you choose the size it's drawn at.

### Generate mesh from image

One click turns an image into a mesh: Rigby2D traces the art, places the points and builds the triangles, keeping corners sharp. Then bend it.

### Godot runtime, production-ready

RigbySkeleton2D and RigbyScene2D are complete and out of preview, tested against the editor on every frame.

### Also new

- **Your own shortcuts**: rebind any action, keep more than one key for it, and export or import your keymap. The default preset is now called *Spine-like*.
- **Copy and paste keys** in the timeline, across bones and frames, with several regions at once and three paste modes.
- **Layered PNGs in place**: same-size PNGs exported from a drawing land where they were drawn, like PSD layers, ready for AutoRig. AutoRig's spine now finds the neck and head.
- **Opening projects in the browser editor**: an ordinary file picker, or drop the files on the window. Rigby2D finds the project among them and asks only for any file that's missing.
- **Every export and render reports back** in a dialog: where the files went (with *Show in folder* on desktop) or what went wrong. Playback pauses while they run.
- **Phaser 4**: the Phaser runtime runs on Phaser 3.60+ and 4.
- **What's new in 0.5**: a short tour on the first start, also in *Help ▸ What's new*.
- **Support Rigby2D**: a [funding page](https://rigby2d.com/fund/) and *Help ▸ Supporters*.

### Fixes

- Moving a mesh to another bone no longer crashes (#22, #28).
- Bone hover skips bones without a world transform (#25).
- DragonBones import opens the character, not a weapon or bullet listed first.
- The physics section says that physics simulates during playback (#27).
- Auto-save and add-ons work on Linux, where the app data folder was refused (#29).

Everything from 0.4.3 is included: the Cocos Creator runtime and DragonBones projects that match the original.
